- **Strong seasonality:** Monthly median PM2.5 runs from 30 µg/m³ in August to 193 in November, about a 6× swing. The high season is October to February (Oct 92, Nov 193, Dec 180, Jan 159, Feb 103), and the monsoon months (Jul to Sep, 30 to 35) are the cleanest. Use month or season features, and evaluate on several seasons. A model scored only on summer data will look much better than it is.
- **Clear winter diurnal cycle:** Winter median PM2.5 is highest around 22:00 to 23:00 (221 to 222) and lowest around 16:00 (114), roughly a 2× swing within a day. It rises quickly from 18:00 (139) to 21:00 (213), and there is a small morning bump at 08:00 to 10:00 (188 to 195). Hour-of-day features and lagged values should help.
- **Heavy right tail and a likely cap:** The median is 70, the 99th percentile is about 463, the 99.9th about 745, and the maximum is exactly 1000.0. A round maximum like that suggests an instrument or reporting ceiling. Check how many readings equal 1000 and decide whether to flag or clip them. Also consider a log transform or a loss that is robust to outliers.
- **Pollutant missingness is moderate, weather missingness is high:** PM2.5 is missing 6.6% of the time, and NO, NO2, NOx, CO and Ozone are missing 6% to 8%. The weather covariates are missing much more often: rainfall 45.3%, temperature 34.7%, pressure 34.3%, wind speed 24.8%, wind direction 22.4%, solar radiation 23.2% and humidity 21.7%. Benzene (26.9%), toluene (30.4%), NH3 (22.4%) and SO2 (20.6%) are also patchy. Dropping rows with any missing value would lose a large share of the data. Impute, add missingness flags, or leave out the sparsest columns.
- **Station differences are real but not measured here:** The summary names Anand Vihar as the dirtiest station and Sri Aurobindo Marg as the cleanest. It gives no per-station values, so the size of the gap is unknown. Compute per-station distributions before deciding between a single pooled model and station effects.
- **Unknowns to check:** 2,384,352 rows over 34 stations works out to about 70,100 hourly rows per station, or about 8 years if coverage were complete. Coverage per station and missingness by station and time are not given, and neither is the set of months counted as "winter." Confirm all three before splitting the data into train and test sets.
